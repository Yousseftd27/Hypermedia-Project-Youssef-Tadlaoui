PROJECT ANALYSIS:

User profile: Michael, a 25 year old founder of a small tech startup looking to start ambitious projects with few partners. He is looking for talented people with good ideas, experience, and a background of projects involved. Michael is actively looking for partners on LinkedIn and other sites, so he dedicates about 5 minutes to each CV to not miss out any oportunities. He is a software engineer looking for people around his age and with similar backgrounds, and he likes simple, efficient and straight to the point comrades. 

Information architecture: With the structure of the page, I aim to capture Michael’s attention and interest. To achieve that, I decided to introduce myself first, because I’m the one he will be working with on these projects. I start with my name, a picture and a brief line explaining what I’m looking for, which lets Michael know whether it’s worth continuing to read. Also, so that he can contact me at any moment, I’ve put my contact information in a slim header that is always visible. Next, I include the projects I’ve been involved in, because that’s what Michael is interested in: my experience in real projects. He wants people to get straight to the point. After that, once he is interested, he can check the next part: my skills and work style. Then come my studies and language skills, and the page ends with an about me section with extra information about me, followed by my contact information once again. I put the about me section here at the end because it helps him understand my situation, so I don’t think it’s filler. I chose this structure because I think Michael, who doesn’t spend more than 5 minutes on each CV, wouldn’t appreciate one with a lot of filler, one that struggles to get to the point, or one that doesn’t share the person’s projects or experience.

Visual design: For the visual design of the webpage, I decided on a simple and clear page. The reason is that Michael values clarity over decoration because he knows what he's looking for, and if the page is easy to read and includes what he's looking for, it's enough. For the color palette, I went with a classic and simple white, gray and black, and added aquamarine as an accent to highlight some details. For the typography, I've researched a few webpages, and the ones that looked the most professional and clean are Inter and Inria sans, both sans-serif typefaces. For the layout, I've decided to go for a single centered column with a sticky header for my contact information and other things. I think this is the right move because I want him to be able to read it clearly on any device he uses. I don't want to overwhelm Michael with a lot of information side by side. I want to take advantage of my information structure and let him scroll down point by point, in the order I intended and which I think will best capture his attention. Finally, for the sake of clarity, I've also chosen gentler white and black colors to reduce contrast and improve the webpage's readability.
